# Machine Learning Subsystem Audit — AI-JobShield

## Overview
This document details the architectural audit, empirical benchmarks, adversarial evaluations, and risk analysis for the machine learning component within AI-JobShield.

---

## 1. System Specifications & Configuration

### Architecture
- **Framework**: `scikit-learn` Pipeline
- **Vectorizer**: `TfidfVectorizer`
  - `ngram_range`: `(1, 2)` (unigrams + bigrams)
  - `min_df`: `2`
  - `sublinear_tf`: `True`
  - `max_df`: `1.0`
  - `stop_words`: `None`
  - `vocabulary_size`: 956 terms
- **Classifier**: `LogisticRegression`
  - `C`: `1.0`
  - `class_weight`: `'balanced'`
  - `penalty`: `'l2'`
  - `solver`: `'liblinear'`
  - `max_iter`: `1000`
- **Artifact Path**: `backend/models/jobshield_model.joblib`
- **Target Classes**:
  - `0`: Legitimate Job Posting
  - `1`: Fraudulent / Scam Job Posting

## 2. Training Data Integrity & Contamination Analysis

### Training Dataset: `data/demo_training_data.csv`
- **Total Samples**: 400 rows
- **Split**: 200 Scam (50%), 200 Legitimate (50%)
- **Source**: 100% synthetically generated by `ml/generate_demo_data.py`.
- **Underlying Templates**: 18 fixed templates (10 scam, 8 legitimate).

### Critical Vulnerabilities:
1. **Target Leakage via Company Names**:
   - Legitimate company names (Infosys, TCS, Wipro, Google, Amazon) appeared exclusively in legitimate rows.
   - Scam company names (QuickEarn, FastJobs, Apex Solutions) appeared exclusively in scam rows.
   - The linear model assigned large negative coefficients to `infosys is` (-0.398) and `tcs is` (-0.381), causing any job with those company names to receive lower scam probabilities regardless of the actual text.
2. **Boilerplate Memorization**:
   - The synthetic dataset possesses zero lexical diversity. 
   - While the model reports 100% accuracy on its internal 25% test split ($N=100$), its real-world generalization degrades by over 34%.
3. **Training vs Inference Disconnect**:
   - Synthetic training templates include emails and URLs (`contact: hr@gmail.com`).
   - In production inference (`analyzer.py`), only `title`, `company_name`, `description`, and `salary` are passed to the ML service; URLs and emails are analyzed separately by deterministic services.

## 5. Adversarial Testing Summary

1. **Variation A (Legitimate Styles)**:
   - Evaluated 4 styles: Professional, Poor Grammar, Brief, Long Enterprise.
   - All correctly classified as Legitimate ($P(\text{scam})$: 0.2473 to 0.3616).
2. **Variation B (Scam Formulations)**:
   - Obvious scam keywords achieved strong detection ($P(\text{scam}) = 0.8945$).
   - Paraphrased and indirect scams dropped to borderline levels ($P(\text{scam}) \approx 0.5036$ to $0.5605$).
3. **Variation C (Legitimate Roles with Sensitive Words)**:
   - Razorpay Payment Operations Engineer triggered **False Positive** ($P(\text{scam}) = 0.5188$, Pred: 1) due to terms `bank`, `account`, and `bank account`.
   - Amazon Gift Cards role elevated $P(\text{scam})$ to 0.4843.
4. **Variation D (Scam in Legitimate Disguise)**:
   - Copied Google JD with Rs 2,500 background fee resulted in **False Negative** ($P(\text{scam}) = 0.3984$, Pred: 0).
   - Graduate role with Rs 950 proctoring fee resulted in **False Negative** ($P(\text{scam}) = 0.4154$, Pred: 0).

## 6. Mathematical Integration and Risk Protection

In `scoring.py`:
$$\text{scam\_dim\_score} = 0.70 \times \text{rule\_scam\_score} + 0.30 \times \text{ml\_scam\_score}$$
$$\text{trust\_score} = 0.25 S_{\text{comp}} + 0.20 S_{\text{src}} + 0.15 S_{\text{qual}} + 0.30 S_{\text{scam}} + 0.10 S_{\text{cont}}$$

$$\Delta \text{Trust Score} = 0.30 \times 0.30 \times \Delta \text{ml\_scam\_score} = \mathbf{0.09} \times \Delta \text{ml\_scam\_score}$$

- **ML Impact**: The ML score directly influences at most **9 points** of the final trust score.
- **Fail-Safe Mechanism**: Critical evidence hard caps (`IMPERSONATION_RISK \le 25`, `CRITICAL_EVIDENCE \le 35`, `UNVERIFIED \le 65`) guarantee that even if the ML model suffers a 100% false negative ($P(\text{scam}) = 0.0$), the final score remains constrained to high-risk zones.

## 7. Conclusions & Next Step Recommendations

- **Selected Path**: **Option B — Retrain using an improved, real-world, leak-free dataset**.
- **Scope for Step 7**:
  1. Build a multi-source dataset combining EMSCAD and real-world verified scam and legitimate recruitment postings.
  2. Implement entity masking for company names and recruiter contact domains to eradicate leakage.
  3. Include affirmative negative examples of legitimate fintech/banking, crypto, and HR coordination roles.
  4. Calibrate probabilities using Platt scaling or isotonic regression to bring validation Brier score below 0.08.
  5. Preserve the lightweight, sub-5ms Scikit-Learn pipeline.
